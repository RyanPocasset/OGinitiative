Ell Pond old-growth sampling strata

Long & Ell Ponds block, from the RI–CT line east past Long Pond. 2022 RI statewide lidar (USGS 3DEP, about 25 returns/m²) with a 0.5 m bare-earth surface built from its ground returns, 2024 RIGIS leaf-off photo, RIGIS conservation lands and stone walls.

The p. 63 area

Davis lists Ell Pond, Rhode Island’s only National Natural Landmark, as a 10–15 acre rocky ravine sheltering large eastern hemlock (locally “the Cathedral”) and a bog pond ringed by a floating mat, with adelgid already killing many of the hemlocks. On the ground that is the granite trough carrying Ell Pond’s outlet to Long Pond, where the Narragansett Trail drops between two rock walls to the footbridge.

The documented old growth scores low on lidar structure

Inside the rim, canopy tops sit near 16 m and the median structure score is the 15th percentile of the block’s forest (18th within its own landform; 25th in the ravine core). None of it lands in a high-structure stratum. The big hemlock crowns are gone and what is left is short cedar and hemlock on ledge and swamp, so a tall-and-complex canopy index walks past it. That is why all three designs put points in every stratum instead of only the high tier.

Model C adds land-use history and reads the site differently: all of the rim’s upland (33% of the rim) falls in “no field evidence” — no stone wall within 120 m and pitted ground — while 94% of the rim stays in the low structure tier. Field plots in LO-UPN and LO-RGH are the test of whether land use beats canopy height.

Strata and allocation

How the strata were built

  • 10 m cells; forest is any cell whose lidar 95th-percentile height is at least 5 m (1,124 ha of the block).
  • Structure index, all three models: mean percentile rank of 30 m top height, density of crowns taller than the block’s 90th-percentile tree (23.8 m), canopy-height variability, and vertical spread of returns. Each is ranked within canopy type, which cancels most of the leaf-off bias between bare oaks and opaque conifers. Tiers: HI ≥ 85th percentile, MO 50–85, LO below 50.
  • Model B, lidar only: tier ranked across the whole forest × canopy type. Evergreen means leaf-off upper-canopy cover of at least 0.78, a cut that traces the dark conifer patches on the 2024 photo.
  • Model A, lidar + landform: tier re-ranked within each landform, so a ledge stand competes only with ledge stands, × landform. Ledge/steep: mean slope ≥ 15° or cliff faces. Ravine/cove/cool slope: enclosure ≥ 4 m, a hollow at the 50 m scale, or a north-facing slope. Swamp/bog flat: slope ≤ 3° and at least 1 m below the 150 m neighborhood. Everything else is exposed upland.
  • Model C, lidar + landform + land-use history: ledge and ravine merged as rough ground (RGH), swamp flats kept (WET), and upland split by field evidence. Upland with a stone wall within 60 m, or within 120 m on ground smoother than usual for its slope, is UPF (421 ha); the rest is UPN (455 ha). Walls are RIGIS plus 25 high-confidence candidates; pit density is the 0.5 m pit count within 50 m, ranked within 1° slope classes. Tier re-ranked within each group, × group.
  • 0.5 m surface: the 88.6 million lidar ground returns averaged per 0.5 m cell, gaps filled from returns within 4 m; it sits within 1.5 cm (RMSE) of the USGS 1 m DEM. Micro-relief is that surface minus a 6 m Gaussian. Wall candidates come from a 20 m line filter that needs all four 5 m pieces of a line raised above both sides, so a lone boulder can’t trigger it; it recovers 22% of mapped walls in forest. Tip-up pairs are a pit and a mound, each 0.25 m or more, 1–4.5 m apart. The hearth search tested 760 level platforms set into slopes; 4 passed and none is convincing.
  • Frame: forest on RIDEM, TNC, Audubon and Hopkinton Land Trust land (612 ha), at least 20 m from roads and 30 m from the Ell and Long Pond shores, with the whole 30 m block around each point in one stratum.
  • Allocation: 40 primaries per model, in proportion to √area × tier weight (HI 2, MO 1.25, LO 1), at least 3 per stratum, points at least 75 m apart, and up to 3 ordered backups per stratum. Random seed 2026.
  • The block stops at the RI–CT line, 0.8 km west of Ell Pond, because the 2022 RI lidar does. The same scripts run on CT’s 2016 statewide lidar.
Sources: USGS 3DEP RI_Statewide_D22 lidar; RIGIS imagery, conservation lands, stone walls, ponds, roads; trails and pond outlines © OpenStreetMap contributors; site description from Davis, Old Growth in the East, rev. ed., p. 63, and TNC’s Ell Pond Preserve page.
